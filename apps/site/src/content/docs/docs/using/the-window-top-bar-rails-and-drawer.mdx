This page names each region of the desktop window and says what it's for. Read it when you want to
know what a control is, or where to find one.

<Snapshot id="hero" />

| Region | Where it is in the picture | What it holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top bar | Across the top | Where you are, the Node's state, notifications, and the app menu |
| Left rail | Down the left edge | Sources, then your tasks, then **+** for a new task |
| Task | The middle | The open task's panes, side by side |
| Task strip | Along the bottom of the task | The worktree and branch, with buttons to copy each |
| Pane switcher | Down the right edge | The task's panes, run buttons, **Terminal**, then **Archive task** at the bottom |
| Terminal drawer | Under the panes, when it's open | Shells and other terminal sessions in the task's worktree |

## The top bar

From left to right:

- **The first picker.** With projects separate, it's labeled **Project** and lists your projects. With
  workspaces, it picks the workspace. Its last row, **Add project…**, adds a folder on this computer.
- **The second picker**, with workspaces only. It picks the project inside the workspace. It shows only
  where a project matters, such as in a task or the GitHub source, and not on Home.
- **Where you are**, in the middle: the project, then the open task's title.
- **The Node's state.** A dot, green while the Node is live. Hover over it to read the Node's name and
  its state: **Live**, **Refreshing**, **Stale**, **Offline**, **Disabled**, or **Error**.
- **Notifications**, the bell, with a count of what's unread or waiting on you.
  [Notifications and the inbox](/docs/using/notifications-and-the-inbox/) covers it.
- **App menu**, with **Settings**, **Clear cache**, and **Reload window**.

## The left rail

The left rail lists **sources** at the top: places to browse that aren't a task, such as **Home**,
**Git**, **Agents**, **Memory**, and **Archive**. Plugins add more, such as GitHub, Linear, and Docker.
Under the sources come your tasks, one tab each, in an order you can drag. **+** at the bottom creates a
task.

A task's tab carries small marks at its edges, such as a dot while setup runs, a spinner while it's
being archived, or a mark for unread agent replies. Hover over the tab to read what each mark means.
If the project has a rail colour, the tab has a strip of that color down its left side.

### Choose which sources show

1. Open **Settings** > **Plugins** > **Rail and surfaces**.
2. Under **Left rail**, turn each plugin's source on or off. Home and Archive always show.

A hidden source still works. The command palette has an **Open** row for every source, such as
**Open Docker**, and marks a hidden one **hidden from the left rail**. Each plugin's own page in
**Settings** has the same **Show in left rail** switch.

### The workspace rail

Turn on **Settings** > **Appearance** > **Show workspace rail**, or **Show project rail** with projects
separate, for a second rail of icons left of the first, one per workspace. It replaces the top bar's
first picker while it's on. [Workspaces and projects](/docs/using/workspaces-and-projects/#give-a-workspace-an-icon)
covers the icons.

## The pane switcher

The right edge lists the panes this task can show. A highlighted icon is on screen. Click one to show
it, ⌘-click to add it beside the others, and right-click for **Open**, **Open beside**, **Pin**,
**Copy URL**, and **Close**. Under the panes, each of the project's run targets has a button that
starts and stops it, then **Terminal** opens the drawer. **Archive task** is at the bottom. [Panes](/docs/using/panes/) covers the
row.

## The terminal drawer

The terminal drawer opens under the task's panes. Click **Terminal** in the pane switcher, press
<Keys chord="meta+shift+t" />, or run **Show terminal drawer** from the **Terminal** group in the command
palette, to open or close it. **New
terminal** starts a shell in the task's worktree. The drawer also holds the setup script's session, the
project's run targets, and Claude Code or Codex running in a terminal.

## In the terminal

The terminal client draws the same regions in fewer cells: a one-line top bar, a left column of three
framed panels, **Menu**, **Browse**, and **Tasks**, then one pane with a strip of pane labels above it,
and a footer line that says what the keys do. It has no pane switcher rail, no workspace rail, and no
terminal drawer. Its **Menu** lists every source, whatever **Show in left rail** says. [Desktop and
terminal](/docs/start/desktop-and-terminal/) has the rest.

## Limits

- Home and Archive can't be hidden.
- The second picker doesn't show on Home, because Home isn't about one project.
