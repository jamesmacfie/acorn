The panel can carry on a task's agent conversations while you read the page the work is about: a pull
request, an issue, or the app the task is building. This page covers opening a conversation, sending,
answering the agent, and what happens when a send doesn't go through.

## Open a conversation

The panel works on one task at a time, and the bar at its top names it. It opens on the task the page
belongs to, such as the task holding the pull request you're on
([Task chips and handoffs](/docs/browser-extension/task-chips-and-handoffs/#match-a-page-to-its-task)).
Click a task on Home to open another. **Back** goes up a level, from a conversation to its task and from
a task to Home.
The task's ⋯ menu holds **Archive task** and **Delete task**. Each asks again on the first click and
acts on the second. A task with uncommitted changes in its worktree stays, with the reason shown;
archive it from the desktop app to discard them.

- The task's **Agents** tab lists its conversations under **Managed sessions**, each with its state as
  an icon, its agent and model, and how long ago it last changed. **Workflow runs** and **Inline
  chats** sit below as folds, closed until you open them. Requests waiting on you in this task head the
  tab. Click a conversation to open it.
- **New chat**, at the right of **Managed sessions**, starts one, with the same choices as the desktop
  app's **New**: each harness or named login, then each custom agent. A task with no conversation shows
  **Start a chat** instead. The new conversation opens with the cursor in its message box.

A conversation opens inside its task, with **Back** and its title under the task's name. Its ⋯ menu
holds **Open in Acorn** and **Copy Acorn link** to open it in the desktop app, and **Archive chat**,
which archives it as the desktop does. It shows the newest 200 events, and **Earlier messages** reads
further back.

Three or more tool calls in a row fold into one line, such as "4 tool calls". Click it to see them. A
call that failed stays in view under the line, which counts it in red.

A dashboard panel or an app the agent made gets a line of its own, which never folds: the panel's
title and what it shows, or the app's name, version, and what changed. **Open in Acorn** shows it
drawn in the desktop app. A panel or app that didn't pass its checks says so in amber.

While the agent works, a line above the message box says **Working**, with **Stop** to end its turn.
While it waits on you, the line says **Waiting for you**.

## Send a message

Type in the box and press <Keys chord="enter" />, or click **Send**. <Keys chord="shift+enter" /> adds
a line. <Keys chord="meta+shift+enter" /> makes the box bigger. Ctrl works in place of ⌘.

Each conversation keeps its own draft, in the browser, until the Node accepts it. Closing the panel,
switching conversation, or losing the connection keeps it. Page text or a page link you add waits in
the draft as a chip ([Send pages, selections, and screenshots](/docs/browser-extension/send-pages-selections-and-screenshots/)).

An open conversation stays open on a page of another task, so you can add that page to it. A line
under the top bar names the page's task, and **Switch** opens it. With no conversation open, a page of
another task opens that task, on the conversation you last had open there, or else the tab you last
used. A page that belongs to no task, reached from the page of the task the panel shows, goes back to
Home, even from a conversation. Going back to the task's page returns you to where you were.

## When a send doesn't go through

If the Node doesn't confirm a send, the panel keeps your draft and the button reads **Retry**. Sending
the same text again can't make a second message: the Node recognizes it and answers with the one it
may already have. Change the text, and it's a new message.

While the Node is away, or the conversation is archived, the panel keeps the draft and turns sending
off. The message box says why.

## Answer the agent

When the agent asks a question or asks permission, the conversation shows it with the same choices the
desktop app offers: buttons for a permission, and a form for questions. Pressing an answer sends it
once. If the Node doesn't confirm it, pressing the same answer again can't answer twice.

A request answered elsewhere, in the desktop app or another browser, shows its outcome here. A kind of
request the panel can't answer is shown in full, with a note to answer it in the acorn app. The panel
never guesses an answer.

## Limits

- The panel shows messages, tool calls by title, and errors. It names a panel or app the agent made,
  but doesn't draw it. It doesn't show a tool's details or subagents' work. Open the conversation in
  the desktop app for those.
- It shows the attachments a message sent as chips, each screenshot with its picture.
- Drafts stay in the browser until sent or cleared. They don't expire.
