This page covers how a reader reaches a plugin's components from the keyboard, and what the kit gives
a screen reader. Read it before you choose components for a view that has to work without a pointer,
which is every view.

A plugin sets none of this. Each component's focus is fixed by the kit, and the keys belong to the
host. Your tree says what a component is, and the host makes it reachable the same way everywhere.

## Focus

Every component has one of six focus roles. A component's page names its role.

A collection keeps its place across a refetch, because the host keys the active and selected item by
the item's own key, not its position.

## What the keys do

- Tab and Shift+Tab move between stops.
- The arrow keys move inside a collection, and Home, End, and the page keys jump in it. `j` and `k`
  move too, where nobody is typing.
- Enter or Space presses a stop: a button's `onPress`, a checkbox's toggle, a select's list, or the
  inside of a `Rectangle`.
- Escape closes a modal or a menu and gives focus back to whatever opened it. Inside a `Rectangle` it
  takes the keys back.
- `Input`, `Textarea`, `Composer`, and `MentionTextarea` take every key typed while they have focus,
  except the few shortcuts that work while typing.

## Keys a plugin can claim

A tree never sees a key. `onKeyDown` and every other key handler are dropped before they reach your
tree, so a component answers to what a key means, such as next or dismiss, and the host maps keys to
that. A plugin claims a chord by binding it to a command in its manifest. For more information, see
[Add commands and keybindings](/docs/build-plugins/ui/add-commands-and-keybindings/).

- A chord needs Cmd, Ctrl, or Option. A letter alone, or with Shift alone, would change what someone
  types.
- A loaded plugin can't bind a chord that works while someone is typing.
- A frame can claim chords to receive while it has focus, in its `claimsKeys`. It can't claim Escape,
  Cmd+K, Cmd+comma, or Cmd+1 to Cmd+9, because those always reach acorn.

## What the kit gives you

- The ARIA each component needs follows from its role: a tab strip is a `tablist`, a dialog is a
  `dialog` that holds focus, and a label names its control.
- Focus rings, reduced motion, and readable line heights come from the kit, in every style pack.
- Nothing is reachable by hover alone. Anything a pointer reveals, focus reveals too, so a row's
  actions show when the row has focus.

## In the terminal

The same roles work in cells. Tab moves between regions on screen, the arrow keys and `h`, `j`, `k`,
and `l` move inside them, Enter presses, and Escape goes back up. A hover tip draws nothing, so the
kit draws `help` text as a line under its label instead. For more information, see
[Keyboard shortcuts](/docs/using/keyboard-shortcuts/).

## Limits

- A tree can't read a key, a pointer, or the window size. A view that needs raw keys, such as a game
  or a canvas, belongs in a frame, whose keys the host hands over on Enter.
- A tip is for extra words. The terminal never shows one, so nothing a reader needs can live in a tip
  alone.
