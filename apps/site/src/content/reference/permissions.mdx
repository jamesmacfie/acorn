A plugin reaches nothing it doesn't ask for. `permissions` has three parts, and acorn enforces each at
the boundary that owns it:

- `permissions.api`: the acorn routes your frames and trees may call through the bridge.
- `permissions.events`: the event channels your Node half and frames may hear.
- `permissions.node`: what your Node half's `ctx` holds, and what its worker may do.

Your own routes, `/v1/p/<id>/`, need no permission. Another plugin's routes are always refused, whatever
your manifest says.

```json manifest
{
  "node": "./node/index.js",
  "permissions": {
    "api": ["core.tasks:read"],
    "events": ["tasks:changed"],
    "node": {
      "core": ["tasks", "git"],
      "net": ["api.acme.example"],
      "env": ["ACME_REGION"]
    }
  }
}
```

## Asked at install, not at use

The person installing your plugin reads its permissions once, in the trust prompt, and accepts them on
each device. acorn doesn't ask again when your code first uses one. That's on purpose: a plugin that
asks in the middle of someone's work gets a yes without a reading. What they accepted is recorded with
your bundle's hash, so a version that asks for more is a new question, and the prompt shows only what
changed.

A plugin's **Permissions** tab in Settings lists the same lines as the trust prompt, in the same groups:

The trust prompt sorts its lines into groups, and the group is a claim about who holds the line:

- **Enforced**: acorn blocks anything not listed. The bridge, the Node's worker, and the host hold these.
- **Declared**: jobs and checks your plugin runs. acorn controls when they run, not what your code does
  once it starts.
- **Web pages**: pages from the internet in a webview. The shell enforces the host list, but the page has
  live network access.
- **Reads** and **Provides**: the data sources your plugin reads and the ones it offers.

## Mistakes

- A facet you leave out of `permissions.node.core` isn't on `ctx.core`, so your first call to it throws a
  `TypeError`. Add the facet rather than checking for it.
- A `permissions.api` scope this acorn doesn't know parses, and the bridge denies every call that needs
  it. Declare only the scopes listed above.
- Hearing another plugin's `plugin:<id>:<verb>` needs that plugin to list the verb in `emits`. If it
  isn't installed, you hear nothing and get no error.
- Ask for `net` hosts rather than `sockets`. `sockets` is unrestricted, and the trust prompt says so.
